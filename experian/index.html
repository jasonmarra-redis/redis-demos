Stage 2: Application Submitted
"Marcus Reeves" applies for a $15,000 credit card
An application hits a national lender's portal at 2:47 AM. On paper it looks clean: valid SSN, recent address, thin but active credit file. The Experian fraud platform has under 10 milliseconds to figure out that Marcus Reeves does not exist.

Why This Application Is Dangerous
On paper, "Marcus Reeves" passes every static check. Valid SSN. Real address. Active tradelines. A traditional decline rule would approve this application.
But synthetic identities are not built in a day. They are cultivated for 18-36 months with small purchases on authorized-user accounts to build a thin but real credit file. Then they bust out for $15K-$50K and disappear.
The cost of getting this wrong: Approve, and Experian's lender client takes a $15K bust-out loss. Decline a real customer, and you generate a false positive that costs more than the fraud itself for 61% of businesses.
Without Redis: Bureau lookup hits the mainframe. Fraud signals from ClearSale, NeuroID, and Resistant AI live in separate systems with separate latencies. By the time the lender stitches them together, the decision window has closed and the application is approved.

Stage 7: Loss Avoidance
Catching fraud is half the value. Not generating false positives is the other half.
Most fraud platforms either over-decline (killing real customers) or under-decline (eating losses). The Redis-powered model uses every signal in the Identity 360 to do both: catch synthetic IDs and let real thin-file customers through.

Decision Logic

Marcus Reeves score0.94 (Decline)
The key insight: A thin-file applicant with a 31-year SSN gap looks identical to a young immigrant building credit, on paper. The difference is in the behavioral and network signals from NeuroID and Resistant AI. Without those, you either approve the synthetic or decline the immigrant. With Redis unifying all three, you get both right.
