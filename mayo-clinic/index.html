Stage 1: The Architecture
Mayo Clinic's Clinical Agent Context Engine
Five tiers form a continuous loop: ingest, context, reason, act, learn. Epic, interop feeds, scanned outside records, and live HL7 telemetry flow into Redis Iris. Iris assembles the right patient context at the right moment and hands it to the clinical reasoning agent. The clinician workstation renders. Every agent interaction across the Patient In Front Of Me workflow draws from one unified context engine, with longitudinal depth on flash and live signals in memory.

The Problem In Plain Terms
A complex follow-up like this requires synthesizing 12 years of longitudinal data across Epic, scanned outside records, interop feeds, and overnight telemetry. The decision-grade question, "how is the diabetes trajectory affecting renal function and what regimen change is indicated," is buried in thousands of data points.
The veracity, depth, and freshness of each data element varies. BMI is stale the moment it's recorded. Diagnoses persist for decades. Last night's eGFR is fresh and decisive. Each piece needs the right hydration policy or the agent will reason on stale signal.
The Iris job: Assemble the patient's longitudinal entity graph, overlay live signals, and hand the reasoning agent a clean, provenance-tagged context package in under 250 ms.
Without Iris: Each agent stitches its own context from raw Epic queries, slow OMOP joins, and stale embeddings. Sub-second freshness is not achievable. The 2-minute prep window becomes 12.

Stage 7: Clinical & Operational Impact
Time given back, access expanded
The Iris context engine compresses what was a 12-minute manual synthesis into a 90-second briefing. Across a Mayo endocrine panel of 14 patients, that's nearly 3 hours of clinician time per day returned to the practice. Time given back is access expanded, which in Mayo's integrated practice is the operational lever that matters.
