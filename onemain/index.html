Stage 1: The architecture
OneMain's Pulse decisioning stack.
Five tiers form a continuous loop: ingest, context, decide, act, learn. The DB2 mainframe, Postgres, Snowflake, and external bureau and fraud signals feed Redis. Pulse decides. The web, branch, and dealer channels render the offer. Every credit, fraud, and next-best-action decision flows through this architecture inside the 7-second loan SLA, with data access under 75 milliseconds, on Active-Active Redis Enterprise across Evansville and London, KY.

Stage 2: Application submit
Daniel applies for a $14,000 personal loan.
A returning OneMain customer submits a loan application from his phone at 8:47 PM. Pulse has roughly seven seconds to surface a personalized, risk-priced offer before he abandons. Postgres-backed legacy paths cannot meet that budget at concurrent volume. Redis can.

Why the 7-second SLA matters
Daniel is a non-prime borrower with options. LendingClub, Best Egg, Upgrade, and Avant are one tap away. Every second past seven, funded-loan conversion drops measurably.
The decision needs to combine bureau pull, internal customer history, fraud screening, risk model scoring, and pricing optimization. That entire chain has to clear in seven seconds, with feature access budgeted under 75 milliseconds.
Without Redis: Postgres p95 reads sit at 50 to 100 ms under concurrent load. A single decision needs dozens of feature lookups. The math does not work, especially at the volume OneMain is pushing through Pulse.
With Redis: 200+ features hydrated from RedisJSON in single-digit milliseconds. The 7-second SLA stops being a constraint and becomes a competitive weapon.
Stage 3: Ingest
OneMain's source systems flow into Redis.
RDI continuously syncs DB2, Postgres, and Snowflake into Redis using YAML transformation pipelines, no custom sync code. Featureform orchestrates ML features from Snowflake into the Redis online store. Bureau and fraud signals stream through Kafka. The result is a single live decisioning fabric.

Stage 7: Risk-adjusted NIM
Pricing meets the loss curve.
OneMain optimizes for risk-adjusted net interest margin, not just APR. The pricing engine balances accept probability against expected loss, prepayment risk, and customer LTV. Returning-customer discounts and behavioral signals shift the offer down from rack rate where the math says it should.

The key insight: A 180 bps discount looks like leakage on a static spreadsheet. In the model, it lifts predicted accept from 51% to 71%, which adds $2,840 of risk-adjusted NIM on this single loan. Multiplied across millions of applications, this is the difference between Pulse paying for itself and Pulse becoming a P&L line.
